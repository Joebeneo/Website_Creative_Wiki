Backstory & Lore

Nova grew up in a small coastal town where the nights were clear and the skies were spectacular. From the age of six, she would drag an old folding chair onto the roof of her family's house and stare up at the stars for hours, scribbling questions into a battered notebook: Why do they move? What are they made of? Can we reach them?

Her parents were both practical people — a baker and a boat repairman — who loved her dearly but never quite understood the burning need she had to build things. By age twelve, she had disassembled and reassembled every appliance in the house. By fourteen, she had rigged the kitchen oven to double as a furnace for casting metal. Her mother's reaction is still a family legend.

The Workshop Years

At seventeen, Nova won a regional science competition with a prototype solar-powered navigation device she built from salvaged parts. The prize was a scholarship to a prestigious technical school in the city. She packed a single bag and never looked back.

The school was a revelation — and a disappointment. She was brilliant, everyone agreed. But her ideas were too wild, too impractical, too far ahead of what anyone was interested in funding. She clashed constantly with professors who told her to be more realistic. After two years, she dropped out and rented a warehouse on the outskirts of the city. The workshop was born.

Present Day

Now twenty-two, Nova works alone in her chaotic workshop, surviving on contract repairs and the occasional sale of a clever gadget. She's working on her real project: a device she calls the Heliograph, which she believes can translate starlight into readable data — a kind of cosmic Morse code. Most people think she's eccentric. She prefers the word visionary.

Personality

Scattered & Forgetful
Fiercely Independent

Warm-Hearted

Nova is the kind of person who asks "but why does it work that way?" about everything, including things that everyone else stopped questioning years ago. She's warm and easy to talk to, but she can disappear into her own head mid-conversation, suddenly struck by a new idea that demands immediate scribbling. She cares deeply about people, even if she sometimes forgets to eat, sleep, or respond to messages for three days.
